# We're not a tour company any more. That's the point.

I'm Andrew Fife. I'm a registered South African tourist guide — registration **WC7622**, issued by the Western Cape Government — and I've been driving the Cape **since 2012**.

From **2018 to 2022** I ran Prime Tours as a tour operator: the Peninsula route, the Winelands, Cape Point, the city circuit, mostly for visitors from Britain, Europe and the States. I both drove and ran it. I closed the company in 2022 and went travelling, and these days I'm seeing rather more of the world outside South Africa.

I don't take guiding work in Cape Town any more. **You cannot book me, and I won't be your guide.** What I have instead is an unusual amount of specific knowledge: which operators actually arrive when they say they will, which viewpoints are ruined by eleven o'clock, which "full day" tours turn out to be four hours of driving, and which of the expensive ones are genuinely worth the money.

This site is that knowledge, written down.

## Why trust a site that earns commission?

Most Cape Town tour content is written by one of two groups: operators selling their own tours, or writers who have never been here. The first can't tell you their tour isn't the right one for you. The second can't tell you anything that isn't already on the operator's own page.

I'm in a third position, and it's the useful one. I hold a current guide registration, so the knowledge is real and the credential is checkable. But I have no tour to sell you and no operator to protect — so when something isn't worth doing, I can simply say so.

That's not a marketing line. It has a cost, and I'd rather be straight about it: I earn a commission when you book through this site, so every time I tell you a tour isn't worth it, I'm talking myself out of money. I think that trade is worth making. A site that only ever said yes would be worth nothing to you, and you'd never come back.

## What Prime Tours is now

An independent guide to the tours worth taking in Cape Town and, over time, the rest of South Africa.

For each experience we cover, you'll find what it actually costs — including the fees most operators leave out of the headline price — how long it really takes, when to go, who it suits, and where to book it. Bookings happen on GetYourGuide or Viator, not here. We're not in the transaction, and we don't handle your money.

We're starting with the six Cape Town experiences visitors ask about most, and expanding from there.

## How this connects to the old Prime Tours

Same name, same person, different job.

Between 2018 and 2022, Prime Tours sold you a tour. It doesn't any more, and nothing on this site should suggest otherwise. If you've landed here from an old link or an old business card, that's the change: the company that used to drive you around now tells you who does it best.

The operating years are why the site exists at all. Running tours teaches you things that no amount of research does — how the queues move, which weather actually ruins a day, what a good guide does differently. That's what's being written down here.

## What I still get wrong

Prices change. Operators change hands. A tour that was excellent in March can be poor by September if the good guide leaves.

Every page carries a "last verified" date so you can see how fresh it is, and I re-check the six main experiences seasonally. If you've done one of these tours recently and my write-up doesn't match what you found, please tell me — corrections from readers are the fastest way this site stays honest.

## Notes for review

**Facts used, all confirmed by you this session:** name; WC7622 current, Western Cape Government; driving the Cape from 2012; Prime Tours 2018–2022; closed in 2022 to go travelling; both drove and ran the business; based outside South Africa, country not named; takes no guiding work.

**Three things to check:**

1. **"You cannot book me, and I won't be your guide."** Deliberately blunt, and placed early. It is the single most important sentence on the page for avoiding the operator-impersonation problem. Soften it if you like, but don't remove it.
2. **"I'm talking myself out of money."** Naming the conflict directly is what makes the honesty claim credible rather than asserted. It reads as confident rather than defensive, but it is your voice, so check it sounds like you.
3. **The corrections invitation** at the end commits you to acting on reader feedback. Fine if you mean it; cut it if you'd rather not have the inbox.

**Still needed before publishing:**

- **A photograph** — on location, not a studio headshot (`identity.md` §5). This page fails without one.
- **Contact page URL** for the final link.
- **Optional:** an approximate trip count. "Somewhere past 300 runs down the Peninsula" would sit well in paragraph two and makes the experience concrete rather than assertable.
