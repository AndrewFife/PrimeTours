## Buy your cable car ticket direct

This is the most useful thing on the page, so it goes first.

The Table Mountain Aerial Cableway sells through **one official agency: Webtickets.** Its own terms are unambiguous:

> "Cableway tickets are only valid if purchased via Webtickets. Cableway tickets bought via any other websites (other than Webtickets) are not valid."

The only other recognised outlet is **City Sightseeing South Africa**. Third-party ticket sales are not permitted at the Lower or Upper Stations at all.

**A guided tour that includes the cable car is a different thing, and it's legitimate.** Registered tour operators hold dedicated Webtickets accounts and buy through the official channel, so the ticket in your guide's hand is a real one. That's the normal arrangement and there's nothing to worry about.

What to be wary of is a **standalone ticket sold to you by some other website**. If you're buying a ticket rather than a tour, buy it from Webtickets.

## Does an online ticket skip the queue?

**No.** This is the single most common misunderstanding about Table Mountain, and the Cableway says so plainly on its own site: *"Please remember that you will still need to queue for the Cableway on busy days."*

An online ticket saves you money and saves you the ticket-office queue. It does not save you the **cable car** queue, which is the one that eats your morning.

The only thing that genuinely skips it is a **Fast Track ticket at R1,200** — two and a half times the standard fare. Whether that's worth it depends entirely on how busy the day is, which brings us to the real answer.

<div class="pt-verdict pt-verdict--yes">
<span class="pt-verdict__label">Verdict — yes, but the hour you go decides everything</span>

Table Mountain is genuinely one of the great views, and no, it isn't overrated. It is also the single easiest Cape Town experience to ruin by arriving at the wrong time.

**Go on the first or second car of the day.** The Cableway opens at 08:30 in summer. Arrive for opening and you'll be up in minutes, with soft light and a mountain that isn't yet full of people. By late morning in high season you can be looking at a long wait in full sun with no shade.

**In December and January the queue can be terrible by 11am.** Not slow — terrible. That is peak season, peak wind season and school holidays all at once, and it is the difference between a lovely morning and standing on tarmac for an hour wondering why you bothered.

The paradox worth understanding: **the days with the best visibility are the busiest days.** Everyone in Cape Town can see the mountain is clear, and everyone goes. If you wake up to a perfect still morning, that's your cue to leave immediately, not to have a leisurely breakfast first.

Which puts Fast Track in perspective. R1,200 buys you past a queue that going early avoids for nothing. If you genuinely cannot get there for opening — a cruise day, a tight itinerary — it may be worth it in high summer. Otherwise, set an alarm and keep the R725.

## The weather will decide for you

The Cableway closes in high wind, and it closes often. There is no negotiating with this.

- **Check [tablemountain.net](https://www.tablemountain.net/) the morning of your visit.** The homepage shows live status, current conditions and waiting times at both stations.
- The Cableway also publishes an **app** with live status, which is worth installing for the few days you're in Cape Town.
- **A closed Cableway isn't a lost ticket.** Tickets are valid seven days, so you can use it on the next open day that week, or claim a refund online.

That last point matters more than it sounds: it means you can safely book online in advance for the saving, without gambling on the weather.

**The tablecloth is not the same as bad weather.** That famous cloud pouring over the top usually means the south-easter is blowing — which frequently means the Cableway is shut, even though the city below is sunny and beautiful. It's the wind that closes it, not rain.

**Best overall: March and April.** The south-easter has dropped, visibility is at its best, and the summer crowds have gone home. December and January have the longest days and the worst combination of wind and queues.

Northern-hemisphere visitors routinely get this backwards: **December is high summer here**, and it is also the windiest time of year.

## Should you hike up instead?

**Platteklip Gorge is a hike, not a walk** — and that distinction is the whole point.

As hikes go it's **moderate**: two to three hours up the front of the mountain, steep and continuous, but no scrambling and nothing technical. If you hike, you'll be fine.

The catch is that "moderate" is a hiker's word. It assumes you know what two hours of sustained uphill feels like, that you'll pace yourself, and that you'll turn back if the day turns against you. People who don't hike read "moderate" as "manageable" and set off at midday in trainers with one bottle of water. That's where it goes wrong — not the difficulty of the trail, but the gap between what the grading means and what a non-hiker hears.

So: **if you hike, do it.** It's a genuinely good route and a better arrival at the top than the cable car gives you. If you don't hike, a holiday isn't the time to start.

A sensible plan for those up to it: **hike up, ride down.** A one-way cable car ticket is R300.

Two practical points either way. Start early — the gorge holds heat and offers almost no shade, so it's a morning job. And **if the Cableway closes while you're at the top, you walk down**, which is harder on the knees than the way up and needs daylight in hand.

## Getting there — don't take your own car

This is the practical detail that catches people out, and almost nobody mentions it.

**Parking at the Lower Cableway Station is a nightmare**, particularly in season and particularly on the clear mornings everyone else has also chosen. Tafelberg Road is narrow, the official parking fills early, and the overflow means walking uphill from wherever you eventually squeeze in — before you've even joined the queue.

**Take a taxi, or the MyCiTi bus.** Both drop you at the station, both remove the whole problem, and a taxi split between two or more people costs less than the aggravation is worth. Check the MyCiTi site for current routes and times before relying on it.

If you're on a guided tour, this is moot — the operator handles it, which is quietly one of the better arguments for the combined tour.

## Is the city tour worth adding?

Most visitors combine the mountain with a half-day city tour, and the pairing makes sense — the Cableway takes about two hours all in, which doesn't fill a day.

A standard half-day city tour covers the **Company's Garden**, the **Castle of Good Hope**, **Bo-Kaap**, **Greenmarket Square** and **Signal Hill**, usually with hotel pickup, in about four hours.

**Check the group size before you book.** It's the single biggest difference between a good half-day city tour and a forgettable one, and it's easy to miss because the listings rarely lead with it. Twelve people in a minibus is a guide answering your questions. Fifty on a coach is a commentary you listen to. The tour we recommend below caps at twelve — that isn't incidental, it's most of why it rates as well as it does.

### Two things nobody mentions about these tours

**The cable car ticket is almost never included.** We checked every major half-day city tour on both booking platforms. One lists "cable car fees" under *exclusions* while selling Table Mountain in its title; another states plainly that the cableway ticket is "at guests own expense". So a R1,280 tour is really an R1,755 day. Read the inclusions line, not the title.

**There is nearly always a shopping stop.** A "diamond factory", a "diamond museum", a gemstone "experience" — the wording varies, the arrangement doesn't. These are retail stops that pay commission to the operator, dressed as sightseeing. It's twenty minutes of your morning and it's on almost every itinerary in the category. Not a scandal, but you should know before you're standing in one.

**The flexibility argument matters here.** Doing the Cableway independently means you can go the moment the weather is right. Booking it inside a tour means going when the tour goes — and if that day is windy, the mountain portion may simply not happen.

For a first visit with several days in Cape Town, the sequence I'd suggest is: **book a city tour for any day, and keep the mountain flexible for the best morning you get.**

**On Bo-Kaap:** it is a residential neighbourhood, not an attraction. People live in those houses. A good guide will tell you where to photograph and where not to; a poor one will let a group treat someone's front step as a backdrop. Worth choosing an operator that visits with a guide rather than simply parking a bus there.

## Practical questions

**How long do I need at the top?** An hour is enough to walk the three short paths and take the photographs. Two if you want to sit with a coffee and let it sink in.

**Is it accessible?** The cable car and the paths at the top are wheelchair accessible. The Cableway publishes detail on this, and it's genuinely good.

**Do the cars rotate?** Yes — the floor turns through 360° on the way up, so everyone gets the whole view without jostling. It takes about five minutes.

**What should I wear?** It is materially colder and windier at the top than at the bottom. Take a layer even on a hot day; people are caught out by this constantly.

**Can I book the cable car through a tour operator?** Yes. Registered operators buy through dedicated Webtickets accounts, so a cable car ticket included in a guided tour is a legitimate one. What to avoid is a standalone ticket resold by an unrelated website. See the top of this page.
