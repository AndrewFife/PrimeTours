# Editorial standards

How this site decides what to tell you.

## Who writes it

One person: Andrew Fife, a registered South African tourist guide (WC7622) who drove these routes from 2012 and ran Prime Tours as an operator from 2018 to 2022.

Every substantial page carries a byline naming him and stating which experience it draws on. Where a page covers something he has not personally done recently, it says so rather than implying otherwise. **We don't publish anonymous brand-voice content**, and we don't pretend to first-hand knowledge we don't have.

## How do we decide what to recommend?

Four things, in order of weight:

**1. First-hand experience of the route.** Whether the timings work, where the day drags, what the stops are actually like at the hour a tour arrives.

**2. What the operator's own listing tells us.** Departure times, group size, inclusions — particularly what is *excluded*. Park entry fees are the usual omission, and they can add substantially to what you pay.

**3. The depth of the review record.** A tour with 4,000 reviews at 4.8 tells you something a tour with eleven reviews at 5.0 does not. We read the negative ones, because they're where the pattern shows.

**4. Cancellation terms and price.** Both matter, and neither outweighs the first three.

**What carries no weight: the commission rate.** Our booking partners pay roughly the same, so it isn't a differentiator. See [how we make money](/how-we-make-money/).

## What gets an operator dropped

We stop recommending an operator, and say why, if we find:

- **A pattern of not turning up**, or of cancelling at short notice without refunding properly
- **Advertised inclusions that aren't delivered** — a "full day" that runs five hours, a stop quietly cut
- **Prices that hide unavoidable costs** in a way likely to mislead a first-time visitor
- **Unsafe practice**, including with vehicles, driving, or wildlife
- **Disrespectful conduct at heritage sites** or in the communities tours pass through

One bad review is not a pattern. Repeated, consistent reports are.

## When we say don't bother

We publish negative verdicts, including on tours we would earn commission from. This is not a pose — it is the only thing that makes the positive verdicts mean anything.

If we think an experience is overpriced, over-hyped, or better done independently, the page says so plainly and explains the alternative. If a tour is good but wrong for a particular kind of visitor, we say who should skip it.

## Keeping it current

Travel content rots quickly. Prices rise, roads close, operators change hands, and a good guide leaving can change a tour within a season.

- Every page shows a **"last verified" date**. If it looks old, treat the detail with suspicion.
- The **six main experiences are re-checked seasonally**, and prices whenever we notice a change.
- **Park fees and tolls are checked against the official source**, never against another travel site.
- When something material changes, we update the page and move the verification date — we don't leave stale figures sitting there looking authoritative.

## Corrections

We get things wrong. When we do, we fix it visibly.

Substantial corrections are noted on the page rather than silently edited out. If you've done one of these tours recently and our write-up doesn't match what you found, please tell us — reader corrections are the fastest way this stays accurate.

## Sources

Facts come from the organisation responsible for them wherever possible: SANParks for conservation fees, the toll concessionaire for toll rates, the operator's own listing for departure times and inclusions. Where a claim comes from experience rather than a document, we say so.

We link to our sources so you can check.

## What we won't do

- **Accept payment for coverage, ranking, or the removal of criticism.**
- **Publish AI-written content presented as first-hand experience.** Drafting tools are used in producing this site; every factual and experiential claim is verified by Andrew before it publishes, and nothing goes live unchecked.
- **Use stock photography as though it were ours.**
- **Recycle operators' marketing copy** as description.

## Get in touch

Corrections, disputes, or a standard you think we've failed: [get in touch](/contact/).

## Notes for review

**This page is a set of promises. Only keep the ones you'll hold to** — a published standard you visibly breach is worse than not publishing one.

**The commitments made on your behalf:**

1. **Seasonal re-verification of the six main experiences.** Four rounds a year. Realistic, but it's real work.
2. **Corrections noted visibly, not silently edited.** Standard practice for credible publications; slightly more effort than quiet fixes.
3. **Negative verdicts published on commission-earning tours.** Already the strategy, stated here as policy.
4. **Sources linked.** Already doing this.
5. **The AI disclosure.** I've written it as: drafting tools are used, every factual and experiential claim is verified by you before publishing. That is exactly what the pipeline does, and stating it plainly is better than staying silent and being asked later — but it is a judgement call, and some publishers prefer not to raise it. Cut it if you'd rather. **Do not, however, keep it and then publish unverified content.**

**One to decide:** the "what gets an operator dropped" list is deliberately specific. Specificity makes it credible, but it also means someone could hold you to it. The vaguer alternative — "we stop recommending operators who let readers down" — is safer and much weaker. I'd keep the specific version.
