# Contact

## Can you book a tour for me?

No. We don't sell tours, take bookings, or handle payments, and Andrew no longer takes guiding work.

**If you have already booked**, your contract is with the tour operator and the platform you booked through. They hold your money and your booking, and they're the only ones who can change it:

- **Booked through GetYourGuide?** Use their app or [customer service](https://www.getyourguide.com/contact/) — they respond faster than email.
- **Booked through Viator?** Same — their help centre and booking reference will resolve it far quicker than we can.
- **Problem on the day itself?** Contact the operator directly; their number is on your booking confirmation.

We can't intervene in a booking, chase a refund, or contact an operator on your behalf. We'd only be forwarding your message and adding a day's delay.

## What we do want to hear about

**Corrections.** If you've done one of these tours recently and our write-up doesn't match what you found — a price that's moved, a stop that's been dropped, a guide who was excellent or wasn't — please tell us. Reader corrections are the fastest way this site stays accurate, and they get acted on.

**Disagreements.** If you think a verdict is wrong, say so. We'd rather argue about it than be quietly wrong.

**Operators behaving badly.** Patterns matter more than one bad day, but we can't spot a pattern without reports. See what gets an operator dropped in our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/).

**What to cover next.** We're expanding beyond the first six experiences, and reader demand shapes the order.

## Are you accepting sponsored content or partnerships?

No. We don't accept payment for coverage, ranking, or the removal of criticism, and we don't accept free tours in exchange for a write-up. See [how we make money](/how-we-make-money/) for how this site is funded.

Operators are welcome to send factual corrections about their own listings. That's not a partnership, it's just accuracy, and we'll check it like anything else.

## Notes for review

**The page leads by saying what it can't do.** That's deliberate. An affiliate travel site inevitably gets "please change my booking" emails from people who don't realise they booked elsewhere, and every one is a frustrated reader plus a wasted reply. Handling it in the first section deflects those cleanly and reinforces the not-an-operator positioning at the same time.

**Decisions needed:**

1. **Form or email address?** I've left `[CONTACT FORM]` as a placeholder. Fluent Forms Lite is in the plugin stack, so a form is the plan — it avoids publishing an address for scrapers. Confirm, and tell me which fields you want beyond name, email, message. A "which page is this about?" field would make corrections much easier to action.

2. **Response expectations.** I've written "we reply to corrections and to anything we've got wrong; general enquiries may not get an individual response." That's honest and sets a floor you can meet. Tighten or loosen as you like, but promise less rather than more.

3. **The old contact details.** The current live site publishes a phone number, a WhatsApp number and a street address in Rosebank. **None of those should carry over.** They're operator contact details, they'd invite exactly the booking enquiries this page is designed to deflect, and the address is no longer relevant. Flagging explicitly because they'll otherwise get migrated out of habit.

4. **Do you want a newsletter signup here?** Not included. It's a reasonable place for one, but it competes with the page's actual job. I'd put it in the footer instead.

**Links verified against pages that exist:** `/about/`, `/how-we-make-money/`, `/editorial-standards/` all drafted. The GetYourGuide and Viator support links are plain outbound links, not affiliate links — no commission, correctly not routed through `/go/`.
