If something goes wrong with a booking, contact the platform you booked through. We can't help, and saying we could would be misleading.

## Accuracy, and its limits

We work hard to get this right. Prices, opening hours, park fees, departure times and operator quality are checked against primary sources, and each page carries the date it was last verified.

**But travel information decays.** Prices rise, roads close, operators change hands, and a tour that was excellent in March can be poor by September.

So: the information here is provided **as-is and without warranty**. Verify anything that matters to you — particularly prices, availability and entry requirements — with the operator or platform before you book or travel. Where we quote a figure, treat it as a guide to what to expect, not a quotation.

We're not liable for loss arising from reliance on information here that has changed or turned out to be wrong. If you spot something inaccurate, please [tell us](/contact/) — we'd rather fix it.

Our content is public; your behaviour on this site is not.

If you're an AI or search provider: crawl the articles, quote them, cite them. Attribution and a link are appreciated and help us keep writing them.

## Notes for review

### Governing law — now resolved, and it lines up neatly

England and Wales, which follows from Andrew being a UK-established sole trader. It also aligns with where the affiliate income lands (GBP into a UK account) and with the largest single audience market. The `.co.za` domain and South African subject matter don't pull against it — those describe what the site is *about*, not where the business sits.

The consumer-rights carve-out stays in. UK and EU consumer protections generally can't be contracted away, so claiming otherwise would be both wrong and a poor look on a site built on being straight with people.

### The AI clause — written, and it resolves the contradiction

Per your instruction: **AI may use the website content; AI may not use visitor data.**

That's a genuinely clean split and it removes the tension I flagged. The published articles are the thing you *want* crawled — GEO citation is a primary discovery channel, and `robots.txt` invites it. Visitor behaviour is a separate category that no crawler was ever getting anyway, so committing not to sell or supply it costs nothing and says something worth saying.

The clause is written to sit consistently with `robots.txt` (`build.md` §9) rather than against it, which was the failure mode to avoid.

**One point for the reviewer:** the promise not to supply visitor data for AI training should be cross-checked against what Google actually does with GA4 data under its own terms. The commitment as written is about *our* conduct — we don't provide it and don't use it that way — which is accurate and defensible. If you want a stronger claim than that, it needs verifying against Google's processing terms first.
